# Reusable registered pen-and-ink and watercolor workflow

These instructions supersede the earlier prompts in this file. They incorporate the
owner-approved Mill, Greystone and First Baptist refinements: broken but descriptive
ink, moderate detail, selective material marks, connected watercolor washes, protected
whites and exact registration for paint reveal.

The original owner-approved red-mill-and-river reference is the **only visual style
reference**. Subject photographs establish architectural facts and actual colors.
Do not substitute a newer app Mill illustration, an earlier draft, Greystone artwork,
a postcard or another illustration for that original master reference. Attach the
original reference to each generation request; if it is unavailable, request it.

Prompts guide generation. Reliable registration requires approval, frozen layers and
deterministic assembly. Apply the stages in order. Composition review and final
watercolor review are distinct checkpoints; artwork approval alone does not authorize
app implementation or publication.

## Landmark brief

Complete this brief and attach it to both generation stages:

```text
LANDMARK BRIEF

Landmark: [NAME].
Primary subject photograph: [IDENTIFY IMAGE].
Required viewpoint: [DESCRIPTION].
Exact canvas: [WIDTH] × [HEIGHT] pixels.
Orientation: [LANDSCAPE / PORTRAIT].

Essential identifying features:
[Silhouette, roof forms, dormers, towers, entrances, window arrangement,
distinctive masonry, timberwork, stairs and other necessary features.]

Supporting context to retain:
[Vegetation, ground, water, rocks, paths or other relevant surroundings.]

Incidental features to omit:
[Explicitly identify unwanted HVAC equipment, pipes, fixtures, wires,
signboards, vehicles or other distractions.]

Features that must remain:
[Identify anything easily mistaken for an incidental object.]

Verified architectural lettering, if required:
[EXACT TEXT AND LOCATION, or OMIT.]

Documented material colors:
Walls: [DESCRIPTION].
Roof: [DESCRIPTION].
Masonry: [DESCRIPTION].
Doors and timber: [DESCRIPTION].
Windows / stained glass: [DESCRIPTION].
Vegetation: [DESCRIPTION].
Water / ground / other context: [DESCRIPTION].

Uncertain or obscured identifying features:
[LIST, or NONE.]

Approved simplifications and prior corrections:
[LIST. These must remain in effect through every later stage.]
```

## Stage one: generate the ink composition

Use this prompt with the completed brief, original style reference and subject photographs:

```text
CREATE STAGE ONE ONLY:
An uncolored architectural pen-and-ink composition of [LANDMARK NAME],
using the attached LANDMARK BRIEF.

REFERENCE AUTHORITY

APPROVED STYLE REFERENCE:
The attached owner-approved red-mill-and-river watercolor illustration.
This is the ONLY visual authority for pen technique and artistic finish.
Interpret the actual pen marks within that illustration without its paint.

SUBJECT PHOTOGRAPHS:
Use only for architecture, proportions, viewpoint, identifying features,
materials and surrounding context.

Do not use other illustrations, earlier drafts or unrelated architectural
sketches as style references.

COMPOSITION AND ARCHITECTURAL ACCURACY

Follow the primary subject photograph and LANDMARK BRIEF.

Preserve the landmark’s recognizable silhouette, major roof forms,
openings, entrances, towers, chimneys, dormers, timberwork and distinctive
construction details.

Keep window and door counts, positions and relative proportions consistent
with the documented visible architecture.

Do not transfer the reference mill’s roof arrangement, windows, perspective,
river or other features onto a different subject.

Do not invent obscured identifying features. Flag significant uncertainty
before resolving it through invention.

Create a complete, self-contained, airy vignette. Make the landmark dominant.
Use supporting context economically.

Retain comfortable clear margins around essential elements.
Do not crop the subject or automatically trim the canvas.

Apply every approved omission and correction in the brief.
Omitted equipment, pipes, fixtures and other distractions must remain absent.

INK CHARACTER: HAND-DRAWN, BROKEN AND DESCRIPTIVE

Use fine, nearly black pen marks with:
- Uneven pressure and naturally varied line weight.
- Selectively interrupted contours.
- Occasional light doubled or searching strokes.
- Short, tapering descriptive marks.
- Slight human irregularity in otherwise credible architectural geometry.
- Concentrated dark accents beneath eaves, in recesses and at overlaps.
- Broad open light surfaces.

The drawing should feel observed and drawn by hand, with enough information
to describe the building clearly.

DETAIL DENSITY

Aim for moderate, selective detail.

Avoid both extremes:
- An immaculate, densely rendered architectural engraving.
- An oversimplified outline with only a few scattered squiggles.

Let important structural details remain clear while secondary material
description becomes intermittent.

Do not make every edge equally strong or every surface equally detailed.

MATERIAL DESCRIPTION

Clapboard or siding:
Use selected groups of short, broken horizontal marks.
Leave substantial wall areas open.
Do not rule continuous parallel lines across every wall.

Roof:
Suggest shingles or tiles through occasional irregular clusters, especially
near dormers, eaves and changes of plane.
Do not cover the whole roof with a uniform grid.

Stone:
Use selected irregular joints, corner accents and small shadow marks.
Suggest construction without tracing every stone.
Avoid evenly scattered oval stones, repetitive dots or a decorative mosaic.

Windows:
Retain recognizable pane divisions and light framing.
Use selected dark recesses for depth.
Keep the penwork slightly irregular rather than mechanically ruled.
Do not simplify away identifying window forms.

Stairs, timberwork and railings:
Preserve believable construction and proportions.
Use varied, slightly uneven strokes.
Do not invent additional railings or ornaments.

Foliage:
Suggest branches and irregular leaf clusters through spontaneous marks.
Keep open spaces within and between clusters.
Avoid exhaustive leaf outlines, dense stippling, repeated scalloped shapes
or foliage reduced to a few unrelated squiggles.

Water, where present:
Use broken directional strokes, selected ripples and restrained foam accents.
Reserve generous white passages.
Avoid dense gray shading, repetitive ripple patterns or a uniformly
rendered reflective surface.

Rocks:
Use a few irregular contours, facets and small contact shadows.

SPECIAL IDENTIFYING DETAILS

If a flag is retained, preserve its pole, attachment and draped shape.
Keep stripes coherent and continuous through the folds.
Do not replace it with a different flag arrangement.

If architectural lettering is required, use only the verified text and
placement in the brief. Do not invent words or add a caption.

SIMPLIFICATION

Remove the explicitly listed incidental objects.
Restore the underlying wall, roof or ground naturally.

Preserve nearby structural boundaries, windows and identifying details.
Do not mistake a building corner for a pipe or remove a chimney that the
brief requires.

EXCLUSIONS

No dense engraving, heavy crosshatching, scratchboard, graphite shading,
gray wash, uniform stippling, thick cartoon outlines, vector-perfect
strokes or photographic edge filters.

Do not compensate for the absence of watercolor by increasing shading,
darkness or detail everywhere.

OUTPUT

Exact [WIDTH] × [HEIGHT] full-canvas PNG.

Nearly black ink and intentional white subject reserves only.
Genuinely transparent exterior.

White architecture, glazing divisions, trim and water highlights are
subject details; do not erase them as background.

No watercolor, colored tint, sepia, paper texture, sky rectangle,
frame, circle, added lettering, signature, watermark, glow or blurred halo.

A flattened generated PNG is an ink draft, not an independently layered master.

STOP for composition review.
Do not generate watercolor until the ink composition and corrections
have been approved.
```

## Stage two: generate watercolor beneath approved ink

Approve the ink, establish and freeze its fixed layers using stage three, then use
this prompt with the completed brief, approved ink, original style reference and
subject photographs:

```text
CREATE STAGE TWO ONLY:
A WATERCOLOR PAINT UNDERLAY for the approved ink composition of
[LANDMARK NAME].

Use the attached LANDMARK BRIEF.

REFERENCE AUTHORITY

1. APPROVED INK:
Exact composition, architecture, viewpoint, scale and placement.

2. APPROVED RED-MILL-AND-RIVER REFERENCE:
The ONLY visual authority for watercolor technique and artistic finish.

3. SUBJECT PHOTOGRAPHS:
Actual material and environmental colors only.

Ignore other illustrations as style references.

FIXED COMPOSITION

Match the approved ink’s exact [WIDTH] × [HEIGHT] canvas.

Preserve every approved subject position, proportion, opening, roof form,
vegetation group, stair, railing, water feature and contextual element.

Do not add objects, change the scene, reposition features or copy the
reference mill’s architecture onto this subject.

Every approved omission remains in effect.
Do not bring back removed equipment, pipes, fixtures, signs or wires.

PAINT ONLY

This is an intermediate watercolor layer, not a newly drawn illustration.

Do not generate:
- Pen strokes or black contours.
- Window grids or architectural outlines.
- Hatching, pencil marks or replacement lettering.
- New structural details.

The unchanged locked ink supplies all precise drawing during assembly.

If the result contains regenerated linework, it is unsuitable as the
final underlay. Do not use that linework to replace or reinforce the master.

WATERCOLOR CHARACTER

Match the reference’s:
- Luminous transparent and translucent pigment.
- Visible granulation within painted passages.
- Uneven pigment strength.
- Irregular dry-brush interruptions.
- Localized pooling and subtle blooms.
- A mixture of firmer and softer paint edges.
- Broad unpainted light reserves.
- Selected richer dark accents.

Use deliberate, varied brush shapes.

Distribute pigment and detail unevenly.
Some passages should merge; others should break apart.
Do not apply the same rough texture or pigment strength everywhere.

Do not fill every outlined surface evenly.
Do not create a distressed-paint pattern or imply architectural damage
where the subject has none.

MATERIAL-SPECIFIC HANDLING

COLORED TIMBER OR SIDING:
Use connected transparent color passages with irregular dry-brush breaks.
Allow warmer, cooler, lighter and richer areas within the documented hue.
Do not paint every board as a separate stripe.

WHITE CLAPBOARD OR LIGHT PLASTER:
Keep broad surfaces predominantly reserved white or very light.
Use a few restrained warm/cool touches near overlaps and changes of plane.
Avoid repeated gray bands, decorative geometric patches or all-over staining.

A white building must remain unmistakably white.
Do not transfer the mill’s red wall color onto it.

STONE:
Use connected gray, slate, buff or ochre washes appropriate to the subject.
Pick out selected stones and shadow accents.
Allow some joints and stones to disappear into larger painted masses.

Avoid isolated similarly sized blobs, white outlines around every stone,
camouflage patterns or a uniformly filled mosaic.

ROOF:
Use the documented roof color with granulating warm/cool variation.
Reserve selective irregular light interruptions.
Avoid a smooth digital gradient or repeated painted shingle outlines.

DOORS AND TIMBER:
Use translucent broken color with selected deeper passages and light reserves.
Avoid uniformly opaque rectangular fills.

WINDOWS AND STAINED GLASS:
Preserve the approved pattern, documented palette and light divisions.
Use varied translucent pigment without redesigning the glass.
Avoid evenly saturated digital jewel colors or new dominant color motifs.

FOLIAGE:
Group irregular pigment dabs into varied, partly connected masses.
Use golden yellow, olive and deeper green only where appropriate.
Keep light gaps and selected deeper accents.
Do not paint every leaf separately or fill all foliage uniformly.

WATER, WHERE PRESENT:
Use broken directional blue, turquoise or green passages appropriate
to the subject.

Keep generous clean white channels between strokes.
Avoid a continuous smooth reflective carpet or neat repeated reflections.

WATERFALLS:
Keep much of the falling water white.
Use selected descending cool strokes and restrained darker accents.

ROCKS AND GROUND:
Group warm and cool passages with reserved light and small contact shadows.
Avoid equally modeling every object.

COLOR DISCIPLINE

Follow the documented material colors in the LANDMARK BRIEF.

Borrow the reference’s pigment handling and contrast rhythm, not its
entire palette indiscriminately.

Use stronger color selectively.
Do not make every building equally pale, equally saturated or equally warm.

No global sepia or green tint, neon saturation, airbrushing,
photographic shading, procedural noise overlay or uniform opaque coverage.

WHITE RESERVES

Unpainted interruptions are intentional parts of the watercolor technique.
Preserve them as light subject areas.

Do not confuse white subject detail with exterior transparency.
Do not fill every gap simply because it falls within a building outline.

BOUNDARIES AND OUTPUT

Stay inside the approved artwork footprint.
Do not add external splashes, a sky or new contextual objects.

Exact [WIDTH] × [HEIGHT] full-canvas paint-only PNG.
Genuinely transparent exterior.

No paper sheet, background rectangle, frame, caption, signature,
watermark, tools, hands, glow or halo.

Stop for deterministic assembly and visual review.
Do not claim exact registration from generative preservation instructions alone.
```

## Stage three: freeze, assemble and verify

These instructions go to the image editor or processing tool. Complete sections A
and B **before** generating watercolor; complete sections C through H afterward.

```text
STAGE THREE: FREEZE, ASSEMBLE AND VERIFY

PURPOSE

Produce registered ink and watercolor exports suitable for paint reveal.

Generative editing may shift or redraw details despite preservation
instructions. Exact registration must come from reusing fixed layers,
not independently generating two finished illustrations.

A. ESTABLISH THE MASTER BEFORE PAINTING

Preserve the approved flattened ink draft unchanged as a provenance source.

Prepare these full-canvas layers:
- INK_MASTER
- SUBJECT_BASE
- WHITE_RESERVES
- ARTWORK_MASK

INK_MASTER:
The approved pen marks separated from the subject’s light base.
Do not move, warp or redraw strokes.

SUBJECT_BASE:
The intended light subject foundation beneath paint and ink.
Do not introduce a paper sheet or background rectangle.

WHITE_RESERVES:
Selective protected trim, glazing divisions and other necessary light details.
Do not blanket entire stained-glass windows with opaque white.
This layer may be empty when no additional protection is needed.

ARTWORK_MASK:
The approved final subject footprint and transparency.
Preserve intentional subject whites and appropriate negative spaces.

Do not use automatic “remove all white” processing as a substitute for
separating subject detail from exterior background.

Inspect the separated ink composite against the approved draft.
Approve the separation before freezing it.

B. FREEZE

Confirm the actual canvas dimensions.

If the generator cannot supply the requested canvas, establish and approve
one supported canvas before freezing the master.

Record dimensions, placement, file hashes and raw pixel hashes for all
four fixed layers.

Freeze their pixel data before watercolor generation.

C. ASSEMBLE BOTH EXPORTS FROM THE SAME LAYERS

TOP:
Unchanged INK_MASTER.

BELOW INK:
Unchanged WHITE_RESERVES.

BELOW RESERVES:
WATERCOLOR_UNDERLAY, present only in the colored version.

BOTTOM:
Unchanged SUBJECT_BASE.

Apply the SAME ARTWORK_MASK as the final alpha channel of both exports.

D. CORRECT PAINT WITHOUT ALTERING THE DRAWING

Reject regenerated linework.

Correct paint placement, coverage or pigment strength beneath the fixed
drawing. Never move or distort the ink to fit generated paint.

Local paint masking or attenuation may protect white walls and trim.
Existing approved pigment may be retained selectively when preserving
a documented stained-glass palette or other established color detail.

Record all deterministic paint adjustments and masks so they can be
reproduced exactly.

Do not automatically reduce opacity across the entire artwork as a
substitute for material-specific watercolor handling.

If architecture or ink must change, return to ink review, establish a new
approved master and recheck the whole registered pair.

E. VISUAL REVIEW

Compare the assembled watercolor against:
- The sole approved red-mill style reference.
- The approved ink composition.
- The factual subject photographs.

Check for:
- Appropriate detail density and visible broken penwork.
- Varied pigment strength and connected washes.
- Reserved whites without excessive repetitive holes.
- Correct actual material colors.
- Preserved window patterns, flags and identifying details.
- No reappearance of deleted incidental fixtures.
- No doubled lines or pigment suggesting invented architecture.
- No accidental white patches from misplaced reserve masks.

Inspect on white, a checkerboard and a darker background to expose
halos, edge residue and transparency mistakes.

F. PAINT REVEAL

Reveal only the watercolor layer.

Keep ink, white reserves, subject base and final mask stationary.

Check 0%, 25%, 50%, 75% and 100% reveal.
No jumping, shifting architecture, moving strokes or changing alpha.

G. EXPORT AND VERIFY

Export lossless PNGs with consistent sRGB interpretation.

Verify:
1. Identical original canvas dimensions.
2. Unchanged hashes for all four frozen layers after painting.
3. Unchanged raw INK_MASTER pixels.
4. Zero differing final alpha pixels between the registered exports.
5. Identical alpha in every reveal checkpoint.
6. The 0% frame equals the ink export.
7. The 100% frame equals the watercolor export.
8. No independent cropping, trimming, rotation, translation or resampling.
9. Acceptable pigment placement at full size and intended app display size.

Shared ink and alpha establish registration; they do not prove that every
generated pigment edge is semantically correct. Visual inspection remains required.

H. DELIVERABLES

Provide:
- Original approved ink draft.
- Four frozen master layers.
- Final watercolor underlay.
- Registered ink PNG.
- Registered watercolor PNG.
- Five reveal checkpoints.
- Before/after review images.
- Saved prompts, adjustment parameters and verification results.
- Layered working document, accurately identified as raster layers
  when the artwork is not vector.

Square display derivatives must use transparent padding only.
Keep the original registered pair unchanged.

For a paint-only revision, show identical ink before and after.
If an already-approved ink revision is also being incorporated, explicitly
label that difference in the comparison.
```

## Localized ink corrections

Use this additional prompt before freezing a revised master:

```text
LOCALIZED INK CORRECTION ONLY

Edit the attached approved composition of [LANDMARK NAME].

Requested changes:
[LIST EXACT OBJECTS OR MARKED AREAS.]

Remove or correct only those items.
Restore the underlying wall, roof, ground or vegetation naturally.

Preserve nearby architectural boundaries and identifying features.
Retain the existing viewpoint, proportions, full canvas, margins,
detail density and broken, uneven pen technique.

Do not add unrelated details or reinterpret the entire scene.
Do not reintroduce previously omitted fixtures.

Keep the artwork ink-only with intentional white subject reserves
and a transparent exterior.

Return a revised composition for approval.
Do not treat generative preservation instructions as proof that
unmarked pixels are unchanged.

After approval, establish and freeze the revised layers before
rebuilding its watercolor counterpart.
```
